Spring Math

A spring rated 2.5 N/mm pushes back with 30 N when it is compressed 12 mm, and stores 180 mJ.

Boundary: arithmetic only Spring Math works out ideal spring arithmetic from the numbers you type. It treats a spring as a straight line from rate and compression. It does not know your real spring, where its rate stops being a straight line, how far it can travel before it bottoms out, how long it will last, or whether a part is safe to build with it, and it gives no verdict on any of that. Every number is your own entry.

Starting examples: a 2.5 N/mm spring at 12 mm, the compression for 30 N, and two springs together.

The force truth

A spring rated 2.5 N/mm pushes back with 30 N when it is compressed 12 mm.

The energy truth

Compressed 12 mm, a spring rated 2.5 N/mm stores 180 mJ.

The travel truth

To push back with 30 N, a spring rated 2.5 N/mm is compressed 12 mm.

The pair truth

Two springs rated 2.5 and 3.5 N/mm add up to 6 N/mm side by side, and give 1.46 N/mm end to end.
